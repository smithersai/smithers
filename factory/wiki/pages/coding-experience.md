# A linear story with recursive detail

This page records product intent and defines the Smithers interactions we want to build.

## Lead with the work people recognize

Present one ordered sequence of Changes. Show a short explanation of what the agent just did and what it expects to do next. Keep predicted edits, actual execution and validation facts separate. A completed procedure must not be rendered as a passing check unless its recorded domain result supports that conclusion.

Keep the selected Change visible when opening detail. Show its atomic commits, read/write predictions and required checks without turning parallel execution into competing product histories. An earlier repair should update that linear story while exposing which later revisions need new evidence.

## Make inspection behave like a debugger

Selecting a turn, Change, check or native execution should establish the context for every detail pane. Expose the recorded source revision, inputs, results, failures, parent/child edges and original event. Preserve the reader's historical cursor; later success must not leak into an earlier view. Keep stable native JJ identity separate from the selected immutable commit revision.

Allow the person to compare the before and after revision of one Change and to return to the compact explanation without losing selection. Recursive graph detail should be available when it explains a dependency or failure, while the compact account remains the normal starting point.

## Put feedback beside its source

A retained prototype should expose its measured before/after source and its unvalidated status. Feedback should retain the originating card, source context, text and actor. A queued message is distinct from an accepted revised plan. The UI should make that distinction visible through the actual subsequent native planning result.

Treat completed implementation, validated, vibed, landed and shipped as different product facts. Each needs its own recorded evidence and its own applicable controls. These recommendations are an intended interaction contract; they do not claim that every control or comparison is already implemented.
