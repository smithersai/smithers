# Conversation timeline

A desktop rail beside the conversation maps recorded asks and answers to transcript anchors. Clicking jumps. Arrow keys, J/K and Home/End move and jump; Escape focuses the transcript entry. The visible band follows scrolling and resize, including in branch conversations. On narrow screens, and in conversation columns below 600 pixels, the rail starts closed behind the conversation's Timeline control (touch, Enter or Space) and opens as a sheet over the chat without narrowing it, so a branch side pane never overlaps its code view. The rail measures wrapped labels and reduces the number of visible entries to fit its available height. Each row reserves that measured height; the position band includes row gaps and partial groups. `@smthrs/ui` supplies `ChatTimeline` and the public `ChatTranscript.rail` slot.

A finished answer longer than its label shows (more than one line, or more than eight words) gets one model summary, made once for every tab, device and person, and synced on the message ([Cost](#cost)). Questions and short answers keep their free labels and cost nothing. The tab that shows the answer launches `timeline/summarize` on the browser durable engine, independently of answering: the conversation's window, which Load earlier widens, never every message the device holds. At most two run at once, across the home and the branch conversation a tab follows, and a tab asks about each revision once. `timeline/SummarizeEntry` claims the answer on the server (`POST /api/timeline/summaries`), then calls `ModelStep` through `/api/model/summary` with the question's first 500 characters, the answer's first 2,000 and 24 output tokens, and records the summary on the message. A claim another tab, device or person holds fails `taken` before any model call; its summary arrives with the message. No reasoning effort is sent: a model that doesn't reason refuses the parameter, so the endpoint decides. The viewer's own model writes the summaries when they saved one ([Your model](personal-model.md#answering), decision 0099), a shared branch's included; the `summary` role is the cheap model's tier, so per-person model tiers can point it at a cheap model. Otherwise, while hosted inference is on, the cheap `summary` role is an Admin's setting (`admin/SetModel`), else the Worker's `SUMMARY_MODEL`; with neither, or with hosted inference off, the endpoint refuses the call `409 no_route` and the step fails `unconfigured`, never retried, and the rail keeps its own labels. A failure every later entry would repeat stops the tab's summaries, in any conversation, until the app opens again: `unconfigured`, `capped` (the person's daily cap), or `unavailable`, which the step answers once its durable retries end (an outage) or at once for a refusal no wait clears (a provider's 402 for credit, or a quota with no reset: `ModelStep` seals it on its first attempt). A rejected output (`invalid`), a policy refusal (`refused`) or a claim someone else holds (`taken`) belongs to its entry, so later entries still run. A load whose summary model can't answer so costs at most two runs. Streaming, failures and waits retain the recorded question subject and append their outcome. The role uses the existing endpoint's permissions; a person's own model's calls are kept in their ledger, and the deployment's in its reservations, metering and quotas.

The summary lives on the message (`messages.summary` and `summary_revision`, migration 0066), so every device that syncs the message shows it, and a rail entry shows it only while its revision matches the message's content: full-content SHA-256 identities, so changed content is a new revision that may be claimed once more. Deleting a message deletes its summary and its claims. Outer zoom levels show their entry counts and make no model calls; group summaries, when they come, are made once when the group closes. Provider acceptance before sealing retains ModelStep's documented at-least-once charge window; a run that crashes after its claim and before its record leaves the free label, never a second call.

`timeline.summary` logs/spans expose role, ready outcome, reason and replay status; `smithers_timeline_summary_calls` counts model calls, including failed calls and rejected output. Provider policy refusals fail `refused`, outages fail `unavailable`, and absent configuration remains `unconfigured`. Projection failures log only `timeline.summary.unavailable` or `timeline.summary.defect`; a missing summary model logs `timeline.summary.unconfigured`. A stopped lane logs `timeline.summary.stopped` once, with the failure's `code`, and counts `smithers_timeline_summary_stops`. `TIMELINE_TELEMETRY` keeps these records through the relay. No transcript text or summaries are logged. The engine journal retains model input/output as private run data rather than telemetry.

The compiled first-party contribution lives in `src/features/timeline`; its intended manifest declares `chat.rail`. Until decision 0017's new Manifest/PluginHost loader lands, chat explicitly mounts the contribution. There is no separate private data interface. [Design and limitations](../design/timeline/DESIGN.md), [interactive mock](../design/timeline/timeline.html).

The grant-store failure regression injects one-millisecond retry timings through `SummarySeat`, preserving six real durable attempts and the safe `unavailable` failure. Hosts that omit the policy retain ModelStep’s production backoffs.

Validation sources: `integration.test.ts` (two tabs on two devices, real routes and Postgres: at most one call per answer), `store.test.ts` (two devices share one call per answer; an idle page whose summary model refuses 402 makes at most two calls on the real durable engine; which failures stop the lane), `summary.test.ts` (bounds, content identities, safe labels, the free rule), `view.test.ts` (states and stale summaries), `engine.test.ts` (real durable engine and replay; claim before the model, record after), `ledger.test.ts` (the route's answers to the step), `worker/TimelineSummaries.test.ts` (one claim per revision, the cap, refusals), UI `chat-timeline.test.tsx` (keys/rendering and recorded accessible names), `e2e/timeline.spec.ts` (scripted app scroll, keys, arriving summaries, phone), and `e2e/timeline-live.spec.ts` (real-model question and linked reply on the deployment account's own branch). The live journey waits for the reply entry to sync after question acceptance, follows its `aria-controls` link, and requires a completed answer containing the requested word, the recorded question name and answered outcome, and visible reply text beyond a generic status. A failed reply fails the journey. Device actions and step chapters record its GIF. Receipts live in the work note; configured coverage is not executed coverage.


## Cost

On smithers.sh on 2026-10-25, with bring-your-own-key live, the test account's own model took 350 summary calls in 50 minutes against 19 answers, about 7 a minute for each open page, and drained its key to 402 at 17:39. Summaries lived only in each device's TanStack DB, and the in-flight set was per tab, so every tab, device and fresh browser context (each e2e test opens one) summarized the whole window again: the ledger shows the same 15 inputs, by token count, in every burst, up to five times a minute.

Now a message's revision costs at most one call, whoever shows it:

- **Free first.** A question's label is its own first words; an answer of one line and at most eight words keeps its label (the question's subject and its outcome). Only longer answers call the model (`reason` `long_answer` or `multiline_answer`).
- **Once.** `timeline_summaries` holds one claim per message revision (primary key), so a second claim from any tab, device or person is told `claimed: false`; only the claimant may record the summary.
- **Capped.** A person's claims in any 24 hours stop at `TIMELINE_SUMMARY_DAILY_CAP` (200 unless set): the route answers `429 capped`, and the tab stops asking.
- **Recorded.** Each claim keeps the person, the reason and when it was recorded, never content; `timeline.summary.claim` logs its outcome (`claimed`, `taken`, `capped`, `recorded` or a refusal), reason, status and duration, and `smithers_timeline_summary_claims` counts them by outcome.

`src/features/timeline/integration.test.ts` proves it on ChatApi's real routes and Postgres with a person's own model: an idle page and a second tab on another device make one call for one answer, and three answers across both tabs make three, where the old code made one per message per device.


## Entry text

`@smthrs/ui`'s `ChatTimelineEntry.label` carries concrete fallback text independently of `summary`: fallback availability never claims the summary model completed. A matching, nonempty, specific summary wins. Old cached state-only summaries are ignored. The summary seat requests 1–8 words naming an object and observed outcome; its validator rejects generic labels such as “Reply sent”. Content identities, private model data and content-free telemetry are unchanged.

When the summary differs, the button's accessible name includes both the visible summary and recorded label. Its question subject and outcome stay identifiable after summarization; grouped buttons keep their group names.

| Entry | Before | Now without a summary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question (`ask`) | Question sent, Working, Not sent or Waiting to send | First eight words, plus queued/not sent when applicable |
| Reply (`answer`) | Reply completed (production report: Reply sent), Reply failed, Working, Needs you or Waiting for reply | Linked question's first words · answered/answering/failed/needs you/queued |
| Run (`flow`) | Not connected to the rail | Recorded flow name · completed/running/failed/needs you/queued |
| TODO draft (`tool`) | Not connected to the rail | Draft title · draft |
| Committed TODO (`tool`) | Not connected to the rail | Title · T number or PR number · recorded step, failure or state |

The conversation query retains the existing `questionId`; replies never borrow a neighboring question or parse assistant prose. An absent question uses the reply's recorded id. The agent-status query retains the existing run's flow name. Drafts join only by `answerId` in the visible conversation. The branch's stack is warmed through the existing TODO service, and committed TODOs are selected by their stored branch. Activating one opens its TODO; keyboard traversal stays on the branch. Draft and run entries jump to transcript anchors; drafts use the same creation order as their cards. No raw tool arguments, secrets service values or provider diagnostics are copied into entries. The catalogued `@smthrs/journal` Redaction scrubs recognized credential forms before excerpts, flow names and summaries render; it is defense in depth, not detection of arbitrary secrets pasted as ordinary prose. This is a private view of the same conversation, not telemetry or a new share.

PR checks are not present on the TODO row; no label claims they passed. Web-page rows remain outside this conversation rail. Arbitrary nested tool calls remain in the run's detail, not new rail entries.
