# 0151 · Every session end wipes the device

2026-11-02 · Accepted; steps 0, 1 and 5 built, steps 1b, 2, 2b, 3 and 4 to build in that order. Supersedes [0023](0023-device-ownership-and-queued-authority.md) rule 2. Expiry, revocation and a person change left open pages rendering the previous person's content, and an offline reload could reopen it. The design, with thirteen review rounds and each finding's resolution, is [design/device-privacy/design.md](../design/device-privacy/design.md).

## Rule

1. **Every session end clears the device, as sign-out does.** Expiry, revocation, sign-out on another device and another person signing in all wipe it. Unsent writes, drafts and in-progress client runs are discarded, and their counts are shown once.
2. **Sessions slide and are capped.** A session lasts 29–30 days after its last session read, and at most 400 days from its start, enforced by the server; the cookie's own expiry decides nothing. From day 393 a non-blocking banner says "Sign in again to keep your session". Signing in again as the same person with a live cookie for that session renews the same session row in place for a fresh 400 days, with no wipe. Any other sign-in (after the deadline, after a revocation, as another person, or with no live cookie) creates a new session, and the device ends.
3. **One revocation latch per realm.** A tab or worker that observes its owner's session end revokes every outward path of its realm in that task: requests, sockets, both tab RPC systems, the collection coordinator's channel, worker factories, host calls and the DOM. A deadline timer is a local prediction: it first holds those paths reversibly for one session read, and only a confirmed end latches.
4. **An offline permit in the owner record.** The device opens offline only with an unrevoked, unexpired permit, issued by a verified activation from a session read dispatched after the latest revocation. Only the leading worker writes it, through generation-checked statements.
5. **Destruction only after confirmation.** Revoking the permit is the provisional refusal. The wipe runs only when a session read sent after the latch confirms the end; an inconclusive read refuses without destroying. A confirmed end is recorded in the device database and blocks every activation until the deletion completes.
6. **Revocation outlives a stuck leader.** While an observed end awaits durable revocation, the observer's exclusive admission lock blocks every activation in the origin. The revoking leader stays a control-only writer until the outcome is final; every later leader commits pending revocations before it asks for admission; a stuck leader's worker is terminated by its tab and replaced by a latch-exempt recovery worker that can only commit revocations.
7. **A re-keyed credential is not an end.** A cookie signed by a configured key for a live session now stamped with another key answers `credential: "stale"`: it authenticates nothing, and the page holds without confirming an end until a current cookie arrives or the person signs in again.
8. **Memory tabs and anonymous tabs are bound.** A memory tab fixes its viewer and deadline from its own session answer before accepting authenticated responses. A tab with no viewer is bound as `@anonymous`, so it never receives a signed-in person's data.
9. **One clock for deadlines.** The server samples `serverNow` with the database's `clock_timestamp()` in the same lookup that reads the session's end, after any renewal; clients derive deadlines as `sentAt + (expiresAt - serverNow)`, and socket leases as `queryStartedLocal + min(60 s, expiresAt - dbNow)`.

## Consequences

No recovery protocol exists. Offline use needs one online activation after updating. Session cookies are set only at sign-in and sign-out. At day 400 a person who has not signed in again is signed out even while active, and the device's unsent changes and drafts are wiped, with counts shown. The signing key rotates with overlap: the issuer is chosen inside the session's insert from durable key state, a cookie authenticates only while its key equals the session's, cessation is read from the database clock after promotion commits, and the deploy refuses to drop any key until the database's time passes cessation + 401 days. The encryption secret is pinned. GitHub tokens replaced by a renewal or orphaned by a lost refresh are revoked through a cleanup queue that never ends the session. Sockets need a single-use viewer ticket and hold a per-frame lease. Frozen or hung renderers, older cached builds, unobserved ends and buffered heap are named limits, not guarantees.

## Rejected

Seal and keep, and a 24-hour outbox exception (four review rounds found freshness, recovery and retention races in every keep-something protocol); an absent-permit migration exception (it cannot tell unmigrated from revoked); a localStorage permit (its read-modify-write is unordered across tabs); a socket subprotocol; retiring keys by the browser's cookie expiry (clock changes, delayed and copied cookies defeat it); treating any same-person sign-in as a continuation (a sign-out elsewhere would then never wipe); a successor session with an inherited lineage and a grace window (revocation did not follow it, and aliases could not prove continuity).
